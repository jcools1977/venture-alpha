Week 2: Education (How Automation Actually Works)

Post 4: Why Most Law Firm Automation Fails
Monday, Day 8 | Problem/Pain Point
I've reviewed 30+ failed automation projects. 95% fail for the same reason: They automate the WRONG things. Attorneys try to automate: ❌ Legal research (AI isn't reliable enough) ❌ Client strategy (you're irreplaceable here) ❌ Court filings (too much liability) What SHOULD you automate? ✅ Data entry (intake → practice management) ✅ Document generation (your templates + AI = speed) ✅ Client communication (status updates, reminders) ✅ Appointment scheduling (stop playing email tag) The rule: Automate REPETITIVE tasks, not JUDGMENT tasks. Your expertise is in legal strategy. Let AI handle the admin work. #LegalTech #Automation

Post 6: The Questions to Ask Before Automating
Friday, Day 12 | Educational/How-To
Before you automate anything, answer these 5 questions: 1. IS IT REPETITIVE? If you do it more than 5 times/month, it's a candidate. 2. IS IT LOW-JUDGMENT? If it requires legal expertise, don't automate it. If it's just data movement, automate. 3. IS IT ERROR-PRONE MANUALLY? Automation eliminates typos, missed steps, and data entry errors. 4. DOES IT INTEGRATE WITH WHAT YOU USE? If it doesn't work with Clio/MyCase/Google Workspace, it's not worth it. 5. WILL IT ACTUALLY SAVE TIME? Time to automate should be < 10% of annual time saved. Example: Task: Client intake data entry Repetitive? Yes (every new client) Low-judgment? Yes (just data movement) Error-prone? Yes (manual copy-paste) Integrates? Yes (Clio API) Time savings? 30 min × 8 clients/month = 4 hours/month = 48 hours/year Build time: 2 hours ROI: 24:1 That's automation worth doing. #LegalAutomation #Efficiency
